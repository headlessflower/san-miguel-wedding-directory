# Blog Agent Guide: Venue Reviews and Problem-Solving Topics

Use this guide whenever researching, outlining, writing, translating, or updating a San Miguel de Allende wedding venue review. The goal is to produce useful, consistent reviews that help couples make decisions—not promotional profiles disguised as reviews.

## Non-negotiable principles

1. Separate **quality**, **fit**, and **verified facts**.
2. Score only qualities that can reasonably be better or worse.
3. Do not penalize a venue for being small, expensive, remote, formal, or specialized. Explain who those characteristics suit.
4. Every score must be supported by evidence and explained in the visible article.
5. Use `N/A` or `Unknown` when evidence is missing. Never convert missing information to a low score.
6. Clearly distinguish verified facts, venue claims, editorial observations, and couple reports.
7. Advertising status must never affect scores, conclusions, or editorial language.
8. Do not fabricate first-person visits, quotes, prices, policies, reviews, or firsthand experience.
9. Time-sensitive details must include an “as of” date and should be rechecked before publication.
10. A useful review names both strengths and tradeoffs and identifies who should—and should not—shortlist the venue.

## Editorial Venue Score

Score each criterion from 1–5 and apply the listed weight.

| Criterion | Weight | What to evaluate |
|---|---:|---|
| Event spaces and atmosphere | 20% | Condition, privacy, visual character, layout, and flow between ceremony, cocktails, dinner, and dancing |
| Guest experience | 15% | Comfort, restrooms, accessibility, shade/climate, lodging access, and ease for children or older guests |
| Logistics | 20% | Load-in, power, vendor workspace, parking/transport, location, event transitions, and operational readiness |
| Weather readiness | 15% | Capacity and quality of rain, heat, cold, and wind contingencies—not merely whether a backup technically exists |
| Service and communication | 15% | Responsiveness, clarity, staffing, coordination, expectation-setting, and problem-solving |
| Pricing and value | 15% | Transparency, inclusions, minimums, mandatory fees, restrictions, and value relative to comparable venues in the same tier |

### Formula

```text
Editorial Venue Score =
  (Event spaces × 0.20) +
  (Guest experience × 0.15) +
  (Logistics × 0.20) +
  (Weather readiness × 0.15) +
  (Service × 0.15) +
  (Pricing and value × 0.15)
```

Round the result to one decimal place. Do not calculate a score until at least four of the six criteria have adequate evidence. If a criterion is `N/A`, redistribute its weight proportionally across the criteria that can be scored.

### Universal score anchors

| Score | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 5 | Exceptional: documented, unusually strong capability with few meaningful limitations |
| 4 | Very good: above average with minor or manageable limitations |
| 3 | Solid: meets normal expectations but has meaningful tradeoffs |
| 2 | Below expectations: important limitations require mitigation |
| 1 | Serious concern: inadequate capability or a material, documented problem |
| N/A | Insufficient reliable evidence; excluded from the calculation |

Use half-points only when the evidence clearly falls between two anchors. Do not create false precision such as `4.17` for an individual criterion.

## Evidence and confidence

Classify every material claim during research:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Verified | Confirmed through a current contract, rate sheet, policy, official page, or direct written confirmation |
| Observed | Documented during an editorial visit; state the visit date |
| Venue-reported | Supplied by the venue but not independently documented |
| Couple-reported | Attributed to an identifiable firsthand account or directly collected review |
| Public-source | Supported by a named, linked publication or authority |
| Unknown | Not confirmed; do not infer |

Assign confidence after completing research:

- **High:** at least 80% of material facts and score inputs are current and verified.
- **Medium:** 60–79% are current and verified.
- **Limited:** below 60%; do not publish a numerical editorial score.

Prefer primary sources for operational facts. A venue's official materials can establish what it claims, but not whether its service is excellent. Use firsthand couple feedback or documented editorial observation for experiential judgments. Do not copy or combine star ratings from other platforms.

## Facts that are not scored

These are comparison and fit attributes. Show them prominently without treating one answer as universally better:

- Seated and cocktail capacity
- Starting price, minimum spend, or price band
- Venue type and visual style
- Distance or drive time from Centro
- Indoor and outdoor spaces
- On-site lodging and room count
- Parking and shuttle requirements
- Wheelchair and step-free access
- Outside catering and vendor policies
- Alcohol and corkage policies
- Live or amplified music rules
- Noise curfew and after-party options
- Ceremony types
- Buyout or minimum-stay requirements
- Pet and child policies

Translate facts into fit statements. For example: “The 80-person limit makes this a strong fit for intimate weddings” rather than “The venue loses points for low capacity.”

## Required review summary

Place a concise verdict near the top of every review.

```markdown
## Review verdict

**Editorial score:** 4.3/5 — Very good  
**Best for:** [two or three specific wedding scenarios]  
**Main strength:** [one decision-relevant advantage]  
**Main tradeoff:** [one meaningful constraint]  
**Data confidence:** High  
**Information checked:** August 2026
```

Follow it with the scorecard:

```markdown
| Criterion | Score |
|---|---:|
| Event spaces and atmosphere | 4.5 |
| Guest experience | 4.5 |
| Logistics | 4.0 |
| Weather readiness | 5.0 |
| Service and communication | 4.5 |
| Pricing and value | 3.5 |
| **Overall** | **4.3/5** |
```

If confidence is limited, replace the number with: `Editorial score: Not yet rated—insufficient verified data.`

## Required article structure

Use descriptive headings that answer real questions. The wording may vary, but every review must cover:

1. **Review verdict** — score, ideal use cases, main strength, main tradeoff, confidence, and date checked.
2. **Venue at a glance** — comparison facts in a table or definition list.
3. **Event spaces and atmosphere** — describe the usable sequence, not only aesthetics.
4. **Guest experience** — evaluate the experience for traveling guests, older adults, children, and people with mobility needs where evidence permits.
5. **Logistics and location** — explain transfers, load-in, vendor operations, and event flow.
6. **Weather plan** — assess whether the backup can hold the same guest count and preserve the planned experience.
7. **Service and communication** — distinguish venue coordination from full wedding planning.
8. **Pricing and value** — identify what is included, what may cost extra, and what remains unknown.
9. **Best suited for** — give three to five concrete wedding scenarios.
10. **Potential drawbacks** — describe constraints and possible mitigations without sensationalism.
11. **Questions to ask before booking** — tailor questions to gaps or risks found during research.
12. **Final verdict** — state who should shortlist it and who should compare alternatives.
13. **Sources and methodology** — link sources and state whether the review included a site visit, direct venue confirmation, or couple interviews.

### Format for scored sections

Use this reasoning pattern for every criterion:

```markdown
## Logistics: 4.0/5

[Evidence-based strengths.] This is useful for [specific wedding scenario].
Couples should account for [specific limitation or unknown]. The venue earns a
4 rather than a 5 because [clear rubric-based reason].
```

The article must provide enough visible evidence for a reader to understand every score without reverse-engineering the rubric.

## Research checklist

Before drafting, attempt to confirm:

- [ ] Current seated capacity with a dance floor
- [ ] Capacity of each ceremony, cocktail, dinner, dancing, and backup space
- [ ] What happens during simultaneous rain—not just the existence of an indoor room
- [ ] Starting price, minimum spend, taxes, service charges, corkage, overtime, and required rentals
- [ ] Included hours, staffing, furniture, food/beverage, setup, cleanup, and security
- [ ] Room count, room-block terms, buyout rules, and minimum stays
- [ ] Distance from Centro and realistic guest transportation plan
- [ ] Parking, shuttle access, and safe late-night departure
- [ ] Step-free routes and accessible restrooms
- [ ] Catering, alcohol, vendor, décor, flame, fireworks, and music policies
- [ ] Curfew and after-party options
- [ ] Power, kitchen, restroom, loading, and vendor workspace infrastructure
- [ ] Venue coordinator responsibilities versus full planner responsibilities
- [ ] Cancellation and force-majeure terms when available
- [ ] Date each important fact was last verified

Record unknowns. They often become the most useful questions in the finished article.

## Editorial and search rules

- Write for a planning decision, not for a keyword count.
- Answer the primary question early, then supply evidence and nuance.
- Use the venue's full name naturally in the title, introduction, verdict, and relevant headings.
- Prefer concrete statements such as “the backup holds 120 seated guests” over “excellent rain plan.”
- Define local terms when useful: `callejoneada`, `mojigangas`, `Parroquia`, `Centro`, and `corkage`.
- Include original comparison, analysis, or decision guidance; do not merely summarize the venue's website.
- Link to the venue listing and relevant vendor categories using `relatedVenues` and `relatedVendorCategories` frontmatter.
- Use descriptive image alt text. Do not stuff keywords into alt text.
- Do not call an editorial score a customer rating.
- Do not add the Editorial Venue Score to `Review` or `AggregateRating` structured data. Only genuine reviews collected directly from users are eligible for that treatment.
- Disclose hosted stays, sponsorships, affiliate relationships, or other material incentives prominently.
- For bilingual versions, translate intent and local terminology carefully; do not mechanically translate keyword phrases.

## Problem-first content strategy

Prioritize topics where a couple has a decision, risk, deadline, cost, or logistical problem. A strong topic typically follows one of these patterns:

- **How do I…?** A process problem
- **What does it cost…?** A budgeting problem
- **Can this work for…?** A fit problem
- **What happens if…?** A risk problem
- **Which is better…?** A comparison problem
- **What do guests need…?** A hospitality problem
- **What am I forgetting…?** A checklist problem

Every article should identify:

1. The searcher's immediate problem.
2. The decision they need to make.
3. The facts or tools required to make it.
4. A concrete next step, checklist, comparison, or shortlist.

## Topic prioritization score

Before assigning an article, rate the topic from 0–2 on each factor:

| Factor | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decision intent | Inspiration only | Early research | Booking or material decision |
| Problem severity | Minor curiosity | Meaningful inconvenience | High cost, risk, or stress |
| Local advantage | Generic answer | Some San Miguel detail | Requires strong local knowledge/data |
| Evidence availability | Mostly unknown | Partial evidence | Strong primary sources or directory data |
| Internal-link value | Isolated | Links to one section | Connects multiple listings/categories |
| Business relevance | Low | Indirect | Leads naturally to venue/vendor discovery |

Prioritize topics scoring 9–12. Improve lower-scoring ideas by narrowing them to a concrete decision or combining them with a stronger guide. This prioritization score is an internal planning tool, not a public quality rating.

## Final pre-publication check

- [ ] The article solves one identifiable planning problem.
- [ ] The verdict appears near the top.
- [ ] Quality scores and fit attributes remain separate.
- [ ] Every numerical score has visible supporting reasoning.
- [ ] Material facts are sourced and dated.
- [ ] Missing information is labeled rather than guessed.
- [ ] Strengths and tradeoffs are both present.
- [ ] The reader can tell whether the venue fits their guest count, budget tier, logistics, and weather needs.
- [ ] The article includes tailored questions or a concrete next step.
- [ ] Titles, headings, description, and links are useful rather than keyword-stuffed.
- [ ] Sponsorships or incentives are disclosed.
- [ ] Editorial scores are not presented as user ratings or marked up as `AggregateRating`.
